# How the World's Strongest Man Is Getting His Squat Back After Getting Hit by a Car

Reigning World's Strongest Man Martins Licis took a small break from filming YouTube videos for a pretty legit reason, and he's back on his channel to tell the tale: he was hit by a car while riding his bike.

[World's Strongest Man Is](https://skin.bairibu.com/blog/4671229672.html)

"I got hit by a car a couple weeks ago. I got stitches in my elbow, really messed up my shoulder. I really haven't been able to anything. I couldn't even do a pushup the last couple weeks because my shoulder's been so messed up," he shared. "No broken bones, knock on wood. My tailbone might be fractured, it hurts quite a bit. I'm going to ask them to do an x-ray. I hope it doesn't mess with my man parts, I'm a little bit nervous about getting an x-ray down there."

His new injuries aside, he has been working with Dr. Aaron Horschig, DPT, of Squat University, to help him recover from injuries. Licis documented a trip to St. Louis, Missouri, to visit Horschig to get evaluated on his squat and how he can improve his range of motion and stability. This was featured on Squat University's YouTube channel.

After a series of warmups, Licis gets to his squat workout with Dr. Horschig. He starts light with 132 pounds and doesn't feel pain in his tailbone, so he increases the weight to 264 pounds. Only one rep hurt, so he goes up to 308 pounds for five reps, with Dr. Horschig watching his form.

"The second one shifted a little bit to the left on the way up," says Dr. Horschig.

"I always find that as I'm warming up with lighter weights, it's always a constant adjustment to find a center," says Licis.

"Yeah, I think doing really slow tempo descents on your first couple warmup sets. A 5 to 10 second descent and really feeling for your position, making that brain-body connection," says Dr. Horschig. "I think that can be helpful, to make sure you feel symmetrical."

Licis demonstrates this with Dr. Horschig watching, and says, "I think that's my favorite now."

Next, they move on to deadlifts to finish up Licis' leg day.

"We did a lot of good work for physical therapy, getting my body to feel better, and of course performing the squats and deadlifts, everything is starting to come together," he says. "I got hit by a car two weeks ago, so I'm in a lot of pain, but it's getting better."
