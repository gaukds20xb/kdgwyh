Having a strong core leads to total body strength, power, and athleticism. And it also slashes your risk of injury. And implementing the use of an ab roller into your fitness routine can help you achieve a rock-solid core.

To clairfy: the major muscles of your core include your transverse abdominis, multifidus, internal and external obliques, erector spinae, diaphragm, pelvic floor muscles, and (of course) your abs, the rectus abdominis. Your minor core muscles include your lats, traps, and (to the surprise of many people) your glutes, according to Trevor Thieme, C.S.C.S.

And using an ab wheel is going to hit all of that.

"The ab wheel gives you a chance to train your entire core (abs, glutes, lower back muscles, and obliques) as a unit," Brett Williams, NASM-CPT certified trainer, writes for Men's Health.

The best thing about the ab wheel? It allows you to train something called "anti-extension."

"Your core, as a unit, is responsible for a quintet of key actions. It helps rotate your torso (as you do during Russian twists)," Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S., says. "It resists rotation, an idea called "anti-rotation," which you fight during Pallof holds. It also braces your spine (think: planks and hollow holds), and it flexes your spine, too (as it does during classic situps."
