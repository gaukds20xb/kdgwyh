# Is It Safe to Have Sex With a UTI?

Should You Have Sex With a UTI?

Sex may not be the first thing on your mind when you get a urinary tract infection (UTI). The common infection can result in irritating symptoms including pain, and burning during urination and having a constant need to pee.

But if you’re still craving intimacy with your partner, sex isn’t entirely out of the question. You just may need to do things a little differently and consider avoiding penetrative sex or certain activities involving your genitals until your UTI symptoms go away.

“You only need to wait until you have a desire for sex,” says Anna Kirby, MD, a clinical associate professor of obstetrics and gynecology at the University of Washington in Seattle.

Can You Have Sex When You Have a UTI?

You absolutely can have sex while you have a UTI, but you might not want to because it can aggravate your symptoms, Dr. Kirby says.

“No one wants to have sex while suffering from a symptomatic UTI,” Kirby says.

To minimize discomfort, it’s best to avoid any sexual activity that involves your genital area at least until your symptoms have cleared up, says Felice Gersh, MD, an obstetrician and gynecologist and director of the Integrative Medical Group of Irvine in California.

“Activities to avoid include vaginal and anal intercourse, fingering, use of intimacy devices like vibrators, and oral sex involving the genitals,” Dr. Gersh says. “These can aggravate symptoms or spread the infection.”

Intimacy doesn’t need to stop completely, however. “Pleasure that doesn’t involve the infected area — like manual or oral pleasuring of your partner, breast play, or massage — is generally safe and can help maintain connection without risking further irritation,” Gersh says.

The Takeaway

There’s no reason for you to put all forms of sexual intimacy on hold while you’re waiting for all of your UTI symptoms to go away.

Your best bet is to focus on sexual activity that avoids penetration or contact with your genitals.

This can minimize the risk of spreading bacteria or making your infection worse.
